# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

React + TypeScript + Vite

## Users

High-school students aged 17 with no prior knowledge of artificial intelligence or neural networks. They use the experience independently and benefit from short explanations, familiar examples, and hands-on experiments.

## Product Purpose

Teach beginners how artificial neural networks receive information, combine it, make predictions, and improve from mistakes. A successful visit takes about 5–10 minutes and leaves students able to explain those ideas simply.

## Positioning

An Arabic-first, interactive learning journey in which students change real model inputs and parameters and immediately see the network's calculated output and learning behavior.

## Operating Context

One self-contained browser experience for desktop and touch devices. Students progress through five short chapters and finish with three playable challenges. The experience must work without paid APIs or external services.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Entirely in clear Modern Standard Arabic, with right-to-left layout and accessible controls.
- Covers five core ideas: inputs, neuron importance, connected neurons, predictions, and training to reduce mistakes.
- Uses real lightweight neural-network calculations; displayed predictions, errors, and training changes must come from the current parameters.
- Advanced terminology is optional and explained in everyday language.
- Challenges provide instructions, hints, instant friendly feedback, retry, and reset behavior.
- Motion supports reduced-motion preferences and never carries essential meaning by itself.

## Evidence on Hand

The supplied project brief is the source for the audience, curriculum, language, accessibility, and implementation requirements. No real student data, training corpus, or performance claims were supplied; any sample classification data must be identified as illustrative.

## Product Principles

- Teach one idea at a time with short, concrete language.
- Let students experiment as soon as an idea is introduced.
- Treat mistakes as useful feedback, never as punishment.
- Keep scientific explanations accurate while avoiding unnecessary mathematics.
- Make the full journey usable without a teacher or prior technical knowledge.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Support keyboard use, touch input, readable contrast, proper Arabic direction and language metadata, and reduced motion. Keep controls understandable without relying on color or animation alone.
